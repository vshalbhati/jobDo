4. Matching & ranking

    
    

      Every run reads the full description of more jobs than it will apply to, scores each from 0 to 100
      against your resume (required skills, years of experience, seniority, job title, education, and
      dealbreakers like a clearance or a language you don't speak), then applies to the highest scores
      first. Jobs below this score are never applied to. Around 60 is a good start.
    

    

      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

  


  

    
5. Daily run, safety and pacing

    

      
    

    

      The daily run only looks at jobs posted in the last 24 hours, newest first, because early applicants
      get seen first. Chrome has to be open with the extension signed in; if it was closed at that time,
      the run starts as soon as Chrome opens, any time before midnight.
    

    

      
      
      
      
    

    

      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
    

  


  

    
6. Company sites

    

      Jobs whose Apply button sends you to the employer's own site. Turning this on also drops the
      Easy Apply filter from the search, so both kinds of job come back. These applications are slower
      and less predictable, which is why they get their own, smaller budget.
    

    

      
      
    

    

      
    


    
Recognised systems

    
Auto: a public one-page form it can fill and submit. Assisted: the site wants an account or a multi-step wizard, so it fills in what it can and leaves the tab for you.

    


    
Sites it does not recognise

    

      
    

    
Filling unrecognised sites also needs your permission in Chrome: allow it on the extension's own settings page.


    
Cover letter

    
Used when a form asks for one. Placeholders: {{title}} {{company}} {{name}} {{years}} {{skills}}. Leave blank and any application that requires a cover letter is handed to you instead.
